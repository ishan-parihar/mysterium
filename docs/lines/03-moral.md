# lines/03 — Moral Line

## 1. Purpose

Specify the Moral line — the capacity to discern *right action* in a
given circumstance. Moral development moves from **egocentric** ("what
is good for me") through **ethnocentric** ("what is good for my group")
to **worldcentric** ("what is good for all humans") and ultimately
**kosmocentric** ("what is good for all sentient beings"). Mysterium's
choice-based combat verbs and dilemma encounters live here.

## 2. Scientific basis

### 2.1 Construct definition

Synthesis:

- **Kohlberg (1981)** — three levels (pre-, conventional, post-conventional)
  with two stages each.
- **Gilligan (1982)** — care-ethics complement to Kohlberg's
  justice-ethics. Both are real; they emphasise different relational
  weights.
- **Haidt (2012)** — Moral Foundations Theory: care, fairness, loyalty,
  authority, sanctity, liberty.
- **Greene (2014)** — dual-process: utilitarian (controlled, FPCN) vs.
  deontological (intuitive, vmPFC).
- **Wilber** — moral altitude as a *line*, distinguishable from
  cognitive altitude.

Mysterium's working definition:

> Moral intelligence is the capacity to (a) recognise that a situation
> has moral content, (b) reason from one's principles to a chosen
> action, (c) integrate justice and care, and (d) extend the moral
> circle as altitude rises.

### 2.2 Validated assessments

| Assessment | Measures |
|---|---|
| Defining Issues Test (DIT-2) | Kohlbergian moral reasoning |
| Moral Judgement Interview | Stage assignment from open response |
| Greene's footbridge / trolley | Dual-process moral cognition |
| Moral Foundations Questionnaire | Haidt's six-foundation profile |

### 2.3 Brain regions

Ventromedial PFC, temporoparietal junction, posterior cingulate, dACC.
The "moral network" is approximately the intersection of theory-of-mind,
default-mode, and cognitive control regions.

## 3. Stage trajectory

| Stage | Kohlberg correspondence | In-game expression |
|---|---|---|
| Infrared | Pre-moral (S0) | No moral choice; reflexive survival |
| Magenta | "Magical morals" — thinks it can control outcomes by ritual | NPCs offer charms; player accepts/rejects ritually |
| Red | Stage 1–2 — punishment-obedience, naïve hedonism | Choose: "kill / spare for advantage"; ego-bound |
| Amber | Stage 3–4 — conformist, law-and-order | Group-loyalty dilemmas; "betray / uphold the order" |
| Orange | Stage 5 — social-contract, universal rights | Universal-rights dilemmas; ends/means tension |
| Green | Stage 6 — universal ethical, contextual | Pluralistic, context-bound dilemmas; cultural relativism risk |
| Teal | Stage 7 — universal-systemic, depth-and-span | Holonic ethics — "what protects the most depth for the most span" |
| Turquoise | Stage 8 — kosmocentric | All-sentient ethics; non-dual compassion |

## 4. Game-design mapping

### 4.1 Assessment modality affinity

**Decision-maker, oath-binder.** Moral-leaning builds gain power from
*kept oaths* and *consistent action*. Breaking a vow breaks the buff.
The combat is not faster or harder-hitting; it is *bound*.

### 4.2 Assessment tasks / defences / skills

| Verb | Task vehicle | Mechanic | Stage gating |
|---|---|---|---|
| Choose Mercy / Justice | Dilemma decision | Locks in a moral mode for combat; modifies damage / mitigation | Red+ |
| Vow | Self-imposed restriction | "I will not strike first" → +damage when broken to defend; restriction-violation breaks vow | Amber+ |
| Pledge to Ally | Reciprocal vow with NPC / co-op partner | Buffs both; failure to honour pledge inflicts dishonour status | Amber+ |
| Just Strike | Damage scales with how morally legible the action is | Striking a fleeing enemy halves; striking a fixated boss × 1.5 | Orange+ |
| Restorative Mercy | Convert defeat into redemption | If player has held vow, slain enemies "return"; cosmetic NPCs in town | Green+ |
| Holonic Calculus | Boss damage scales with depth-and-span analysis | Dilemma during fight: protect breadth (many allies) vs. depth (one critical) | Teal+ |
| Compassion Without Object | Damage modifier scales inversely with player's identification with cause | The less ego-attached, the more clean-cutting | Turquoise |

### 4.3 Module parameters(s) backed

- **Honour** — vow integrity; affects ally trust
- **Conviction** — the moral-choice latency; faster = more aligned
- **Span** — the breadth of the moral circle currently held; gates
  certain encounter types

### 4.4 Cognitive task vehicles

- Dilemma decision (latency + choice)
- Moral-foundation matching (which foundation drives the choice?)
- Vow-violation detection (mid-combat: did the player break the vow?)
- Footbridge-style trade-offs

### 4.5 Depth progression

Moral altitude is the **slowest** line to advance, and intentionally so.
A single dilemma cannot raise altitude — only a *consistent pattern* of
choices does. The line moves in *seasons*, not sessions:

- A "moral season" is ~5 sessions of consistent choice patterns.
- Altitude can advance at season boundaries.
- Single inconsistent choices do not regress; *patterns of inconsistency*
  do.

This pacing reflects the empirical finding that moral development is
the slowest of the lines.

## 5. Enemy / encounter structure

### 5.1 Module-level encounters archetypes

| Stage | Side-character | Encoded test |
|---|---|---|
| Infrared | "Hunger-Beast" — pre-moral; survival dilemma | Resource scarcity choice |
| Magenta | "Trickster-Spirit" — offers a magical bargain | Childlike trust vs. caution |
| Red | "Bandit-Lord" — naked self-interest | "What's in it for you?" |
| Amber | "Ledger-Keeper" — strict-rule enforcer | Rule-bound dilemma; punish or pardon |
| Orange | "Contractor" — utility-maximiser | Trolley-problem variant |
| Green | "Moralist" — weaponises pluralism | Contextual nuance trap |
| Teal | "Holonic Judge" — depth-and-span trade-off | Multi-level harm trade-off |
| Turquoise | "Boundless Witness" — non-dual; choice as energy not action | Choice without grasping |

### 5.2 Cross-line encounterses

- **Moral × Emotional:** "The Pleader" — genuine grief presented with a
  morally compromised request; reading affect alters the right answer.
- **Moral × Interpersonal:** "The Coalition" — three NPCs with
  conflicting needs; the player must distribute a finite resource.
- **Moral × Willpower:** "The Tempter" — offers a one-time gain that
  violates a held vow; vow integrity tested under load.

### 5.3 Main-boss role

In synthesis exams, the **moral phase** typically gates progress to the
*final* phase. The boss presents a stage-defining dilemma. A bad answer
does not lose the fight, but locks the player out of the highest-tier
ending; the dilemma re-presents in subsequent encounters until
patterned consistency emerges.

### 5.4 Shadow encounters

**"The Inquisitor"** — at Amber stage. The fixated rule-keeper who
believes their group-truth is THE truth. The fight is structured around
*following the rules*; doing so costs the player. The win condition is
to *break a rule* in service of a held value — moral altitude advance to
Orange becomes possible.

**"The Equivocator"** — at Green stage. The shadow of pluralism without
spine. The fight refuses to be won; every choice is "valid." The win
condition is to *commit* — to declare a value as load-bearing despite
ambiguity. Moral altitude can advance to Teal.

## 6. Architectural contract

```
const MORAL_VERBS = {
  chooseMercyJustice: { task: 'dilemma_choice',     altitudeMin: 'Red' },
  vow:                { task: 'restriction_lock',    altitudeMin: 'Amber' },
  pledge:             { task: 'reciprocal_vow',      altitudeMin: 'Amber' },
  justStrike:         { task: 'moral_legibility',    altitudeMin: 'Orange' },
  restorativeMercy:   { task: 'vow_integrity',       altitudeMin: 'Green' },
  holonicCalculus:    { task: 'depth_span_tradeoff', altitudeMin: 'Turquoise' },
  compassionNoObject: { task: 'non_attached_choice', altitudeMin: 'White' },
} as const;

interface MoralProfile {
  activeVows:   ReadonlyArray<Vow>;
  patternHistory: ReadonlyArray<MoralChoice>;   // last 50 choices
  consistency:    number;                        // 0..1 over last season
}
```

Moral altitude advancement: pure function of `patternHistory` over the
last ~50 choices, gated by `consistency >= 0.7`.

## 7. Open questions

- **Cultural moral variance.** Haidt's foundations vary across cultures.
  Mysterium must avoid imposing one moral grammar. Default: Foundation
  *weighting* is configurable per player, with a balanced default.
- **Real moral content vs. game moral content.** A trolley-problem
  dilemma in a fantasy RPG is morally lighter than a real one. We must
  not confuse player skill at *game* dilemmas with real-life moral
  capacity. UI wording must keep this distinction live.
- **Punishment of inconsistency.** Inconsistency *patterns* regress
  altitude; this can feel punitive. The narrator's tone matters — it
  should *invite reflection*, never *judge*.

## 8. Principles served

Principles **1, 4, 6** — moral development as a real line, earned over
seasons, with honesty about what the game can and cannot legitimately
claim about the player's real-life moral capacity.
