## 2. Core Mechanic

Long-horizon planning and resource-allocation tasks where the player must organise, prioritise, and sequence actions. The paradox: effective planning at White requires no fixed planner. The game detects whether plans serve a self-image or arise transparently from situational intelligence.

## 3. Shadow Detection

| Shadow | Planning signature |
|---|---|
| 2.1 Grasping-at-Self | Plans that protect or aggrandise a fixed self-image; identity-serving resource allocation |
| 2.2 Aversion-to-Self | Plans that avoid self-implicating commitments; diffuse responsibility; refuse ownership |
| 2.3 Self-Bypassing | Plans framed as 'selfless' that subtly serve self-preservation; spiritual-sounding avoidance of commitment |
| 2.4 Identity-with-Witness | Plans that maintain observer-distance; over-strategising as substitute for participatory engagement |

## 4. Task Architecture

### 4.1 Self-Serving vs. Transparent Planning
- Resource-allocation scenarios where self-interest and situational wisdom diverge
- Transparent planner: allocates based on what the situation requires
- Grasping planner: allocates to maintain self-image or avoid self-threat

### 4.2 Planner-Dissolution Tasks
- Multi-step plans that must be revised when the 'planner's' identity shifts
- How rigidly does the player hold to plans that served a now-dissolved self?
- Genuine transparency: plans update fluidly as context changes

### 4.3 Commitment-Without-Owner
- Tasks requiring firm commitment to a course of action without a self claiming the commitment
- Can the player be decisive and consistent without needing 'I decided this'?
- Grasping: either over-identifies with plans or refuses commitment entirely

## 5. Drive Probing

| Drive | Probe |
|---|---|
| Eros | Do plans reach toward growth even when growth dissolves the planner? |
| Agape | Can the player release plans gracefully when they no longer serve? |
| Agency | Can the player commit decisively without a self owning the decision? |
| Communion | Do plans serve the whole situation rather than a separate self within it? |

## 6. Scoring

- Primary: Self-Serving Allocation Index — degree to which plans protect identity vs. serve situation
- Secondary: Plan-Flexibility Score — adaptation speed when identity-context shifts
- Tertiary: Commitment-Ownership Ratio — firm action without self-claiming
- Longitudinal tracking reveals whether planning transparency generalises

## 7. Catalyst → Experience → Integration

- **Catalyst:** Planning tasks that make the planner visible — who is making these plans and why?
- **Experience:** The felt-sense of plans arising without a planner — intelligent structure without self-grasping
- **Integration:** Residual self-serving in planning becomes visible; visibility allows transparent re-orientation

## 8. Progression

- Entry: Baseline self-serving planning patterns mapped
- Middle: Tasks target specific planning-grasping with increasing subtlety
- Advanced: Plans arise transparently — decisive, coherent, and unowned
- Infinite: Novel planning contexts prevent habituation; theta-decay on regression
