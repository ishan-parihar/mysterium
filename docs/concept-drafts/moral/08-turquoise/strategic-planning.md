## 1. Modality Identity

- **Module:** Moral / White
- **Modality:** Strategic-Planning
- **Unique Axis:** The paradox of structuring moral life without grasping — planning within kosmocentric ethics
- **Core Mechanic:** Resource-allocation and sequencing tasks where the player must organize ethical action without attachment to the plan itself

## 2. What This Measures

At White, the paradox of moral planning: how does one structure compassionate action without grasping at the structure? This modality measures whether the player can plan ethically while remaining transparent to the plan — holding strategy lightly, adapting fluidly, releasing attachment to moral outcomes.

## 3. Shadow Detection

- **2.1 Grasping-at-Rightness:** Over-planning — excessive structure to guarantee moral correctness. Inability to act without a complete ethical framework in place.
- **2.2 Aversion-to-Harm:** Plans that systematically avoid moral risk. Conservative strategies that prioritize the planner's comfort over genuine compassion.
- **2.3 Moral Bypassing:** Refusal to plan — "spontaneity" as avoidance of moral responsibility. Under-structuring that leaves others unsupported.
- **2.4 Moralist-Identity:** Plans that center the planner as moral agent. Strategies designed to demonstrate goodness rather than serve.

## 4. Task Architecture

### 4.1 Compassion Resource Allocation

Limited resources must be distributed across competing moral needs. No allocation is perfect. Scoring measures:
- Kosmocentric breadth (does the plan extend compassion to all?) — 30%
- Lightness of holding (can the plan be revised without distress?) — 30%
- Absence of self-centering (is the planner transparent in the plan?) — 20%
- Adaptive responsiveness (does the plan breathe with changing conditions?) — 20%

### 4.2 Ethical Sequencing Under Uncertainty

Multi-step moral action where outcomes are uncertain. Player must commit to sequences without knowing results — measuring tolerance for moral uncertainty in structured action.

### 4.3 Plan-Release Cycles

Player creates a moral plan, then conditions change. Measures the ease of releasing the plan — or whether grasping at the original structure persists.

## 5. Catalyst → Experience → Integration

- **Catalyst:** Planning tasks that reveal attachment to moral structure and outcomes
- **Experience:** Player notices where planning becomes grasping — where strategy serves identity rather than compassion
- **Integration:** Planning becomes transparent — structure arises and dissolves as needed, without residue

## 6. Drive Probing

| Drive | Probe |
|---|---|
| Eros | Does planning reach toward greater moral inclusion without over-reaching? |
| Agape | Can the player release a plan that no longer serves, without grief? |
| Agency | Does the plan have clarity and decisiveness without rigidity? |
| Communion | Does planning attune to others' needs without losing structural coherence? |

## 7. Scoring & Adaptation

- Plan quality scored by LLM for kosmocentric alignment and structural coherence
- Plan-attachment scored by behavioral response to forced plan-changes (latency, distress markers, revision quality)
- Transparency index = f(plan quality × release ease × adaptive responsiveness)
- Difficulty scales by increasing uncertainty, competing needs, and forced revisions

## 8. Compound Shadow Surfacing

- **Righteous Transparent:** Elegant moral plans that subtly demonstrate the planner's ethical sophistication
- **Compassion Avoider:** Plans that minimize moral risk — conservative strategies avoiding difficult compassion
- **Ethics Performer:** Plans that look kosmocentric but center the planner's moral image
- **Moralist-Identified:** Inability to release a plan once made — the plan becomes part of moral identity
