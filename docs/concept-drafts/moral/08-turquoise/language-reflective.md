## 1. Modality Identity

- **Module:** Moral / White
- **Modality:** Language-Reflective
- **Unique Axis:** Verbal articulation of kosmocentric ethics — how the player talks about morality reveals residual grasping
- **Core Mechanic:** Open-ended moral reflection prompts scored by LLM rubrics for transparency vs. grasping

## 2. What This Measures

Language is the last refuge of moral grasping. A player may act spontaneously but still narrate their ethics in ways that reveal attachment — claiming credit, performing humility, subtly positioning themselves as 'the good one'. This modality listens to HOW the player speaks about morality.

## 3. Shadow Detection

- **2.1 Grasping-at-Rightness:** Language that claims or defends moral correctness. Justification patterns. "I did the right thing because..."
- **2.2 Aversion-to-Harm:** Avoidance of moral complexity in speech. Simplification. Refusal to articulate difficult truths.
- **2.3 Moral Bypassing:** Spiritual language that dismisses ethical nuance. "It's all perfect." "There is no good or evil." — while subtly judging.
- **2.4 Moralist-Identity:** First-person moral positioning. "I always..." "I would never..." Language that constructs a moral self.

## 4. Task Architecture

### 4.1 Moral Articulation Prompts

Player responds to open prompts about ethical situations. LLM scores for:
- Transparency (absence of self-positioning) — 30%
- Complexity tolerance (can hold paradox without resolving) — 25%
- Non-claiming (describes action without ownership) — 25%
- Freshness (each response arises new, not from rehearsed framework) — 20%

### 4.2 Retrospective Narration

After deterministic or scenario tasks, player is invited to reflect on their choices. The reflection itself is the assessment — does narration add grasping to what was spontaneous?

### 4.3 Paradox Holding

Prompts that present genuine moral paradoxes. Scoring measures whether player can articulate without resolving — holding tension rather than collapsing into certainty.

## 5. Catalyst → Experience → Integration

- **Catalyst:** Prompts that invite moral self-narration — creating space for grasping to appear in language
- **Experience:** Player notices their own linguistic patterns of claiming, defending, or performing ethics
- **Integration:** Language becomes transparent — describing without claiming, articulating without positioning

## 6. Drive Probing

| Drive | Probe |
|---|---|
| Eros | Does language reach toward greater moral depth without grasping at it? |
| Agape | Does language release the need to be understood as good? |
| Agency | Does articulation have clarity without rigidity? |
| Communion | Does language attune to shared moral reality without performing attunement? |

## 7. Scoring & Adaptation

- LLM rubrics evaluate linguistic markers of residual grasping (justification, self-reference, certainty markers, avoidance patterns)
- Baseline established from player's natural language style (cross-referenced with Cognitive/Language data)
- Difficulty scales by increasing paradox density and reducing prompt scaffolding
- Longitudinal tracking: does moral language become more transparent over sessions?

## 8. Compound Shadow Surfacing

- **Righteous Transparent:** Articulate, precise moral language that subtly demonstrates the speaker's goodness
- **Compassion Avoider:** Abstract language that avoids naming specific suffering or moral weight
- **Ethics Performer:** Beautiful spiritual language about universal love that lacks embodied specificity
- **Moralist-Identified:** Consistent first-person moral narrative — "I am someone who..."
