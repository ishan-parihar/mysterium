# Cognitive / White — Scenario-Choice Game

> **Modality:** Scenario-Choice (branching dilemmas)  
> **Unique lateral:** Non-dual cognitive dilemmas — situations where deliberation fails and only direct knowing resolves. The choice reveals whether the player operates from transparent cognition or residual grasping.

---

## 1. Core Mechanic

**Dilemmas Beyond Deliberation.** The player faces scenarios that cannot be solved by analysis, integration, or perspective-taking — all of which were sufficient at prior stages. These require direct knowing: paradoxes that dissolve rather than resolve, koans that answer themselves when grasping ceases, situations where any deliberate choice is wrong.

Key scenario types:
- **Paradox scenarios:** Two equally valid, mutually exclusive framings. Deliberation oscillates forever. Direct knowing cuts through without choosing sides.
- **Timing scenarios:** The right action exists only in a specific moment. Deliberation misses the window. Spontaneous response succeeds.
- **Dissolution scenarios:** The scenario itself is the illusion. The "correct" response is to see through the dilemma rather than solve it.
- **Transparent-action scenarios:** Complex situations requiring immediate, multi-factor response from wholeness rather than sequential analysis.

## 2. Shadow Surfacing

### 2.1 Grasping-as-Knowing

Detected when: the player deliberates on paradox scenarios (long response time + analytical choice rationale), attempts to "figure out" dissolution scenarios, or applies frameworks to timing scenarios.

### 2.2 Aversion-to-Knowing

Detected when: the player avoids certain scenario categories, gives superficial responses to scenarios touching blind-spot domains, or consistently "passes" on specific dilemma types.

### 2.3 Spiritual Bypassing

Detected when: the player gives "non-dual" answers that are actually conceptual — e.g., "both and neither" as formula rather than lived response. Detected via consistency checks across structurally identical scenarios.

### 2.4 Identity-with-Knowing

Detected when: responses consistently position a knower — "I would..." rather than describing what arises. Scenarios requiring self-dissolution produce hesitation or reframing that preserves the subject.

## 3. Drive Detection

- **Eros residue:** Compulsive choice of the "higher" or "deeper" option where no hierarchy exists.
- **Agape residue:** Compulsive choice of the "simpler" or "more grounded" option — refusing full complexity.
- **Agency residue:** Choices consistently favor individual action over non-action or receptivity.
- **Communion residue:** Choices consistently favor dissolution into collective over sovereign response.

Healthy White: choices arise from the scenario itself, not from drive-pattern. No systematic bias.

## 4. Progression Arc

**Phase A — Presentation (sessions 1–3):** Scenarios presented without time pressure. Natural response style baselined. Deliberation time, choice patterns, and rationale language recorded.

**Phase B — Compression (sessions 4–7):** Response windows narrow. Deliberation becomes impossible. The player must respond from whatever is available — direct knowing or habitual pattern. The difference becomes visible under time pressure.

**Phase C — Ecological embedding (sessions 8+):** Scenarios arise within ongoing narrative without announcement. The player doesn't know they're in a "dilemma." Direct knowing must be the default mode, not a special state invoked for special occasions.

## 5. Infinite Checkpoint & Theta

- **Checkpoint:** Each scenario is self-contained. Player can exit between any two scenarios.
- **Theta-decay:** Half-life 28 days. Decay manifests as return of deliberation patterns in previously spontaneous scenario-types.
- **Session length:** 3–15 minutes typical. Minimum meaningful session: one scenario.
- **Felt-sense reward:** The relief of not needing to figure things out. Scenarios that once felt impossible become obvious — not through learning but through releasing.
