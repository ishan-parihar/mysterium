# Interpersonal × Turquoise — Social-Cooperative

## 1. Core Mechanic

**Live Integral Relating with Cross-Altitude NPC Partners.** The player works cooperatively with NPC partners who operate at different developmental altitudes. Success requires attuning to each partner's level, facilitating group coherence across altitude differences, and accomplishing shared objectives that no single altitude can solve alone.

Three task families:
- **Holonic Team Coordination:** Lead a team of 3-5 NPCs at different altitudes toward a shared goal. Each NPC contributes uniquely from their stage. The player must attune to each, translate between them, and hold the group field.
- **Cross-Altitude Mentorship Pairs:** Partner with a single NPC at a significantly different altitude. Accomplish a task that requires both altitudes — the player must genuinely learn from the partner's strengths while offering their own.
- **Relational Field Holding:** Multiple NPCs interact with each other (not just the player). The player's role is to hold the relational field — facilitating without controlling, attuning without merging, guiding without imposing.

## 2. Shadow Surfacing

- **DA (Relational Architect):** Manages the team through analysis — assigns roles based on altitude-mapping but doesn't connect with any individual. The team functions but lacks relational warmth. NPCs report feeling 'used efficiently.'
- **DAll (Authentic Pluralist):** Treats all NPCs as equals regardless of altitude — refuses to differentiate roles or approaches. The team underperforms because altitude-specific strengths aren't leveraged. NPCs at lower altitudes feel overwhelmed; those at higher altitudes feel constrained.
- **GA (Premature Merger):** Attempts to merge the team into unity prematurely — 'we're all one team, no differences matter.' Altitude-specific contributions are lost. The team feels warm but accomplishes little.
- **GAll (Integral Relational Fortress):** Coordinates the team perfectly but maintains emotional distance — each NPC is attuned to but none are genuinely joined with. The team succeeds but the player remains isolated within it.

## 3. Catalyst → Experience → Integration

- **Catalyst:** Cooperative tasks that REQUIRE genuine cross-altitude attunement — they cannot be solved by analysis alone, by flattening, by merging, or by distance. Only integral relating produces optimal outcomes.
- **Experience:** The player feels what it's like to hold a holonic team — the simultaneous demands of attuning to multiple altitudes while maintaining centre and facilitating coherence.
- **Integration:** Repeated cooperative play builds the relational muscles. The player's attunement becomes more natural, their centre more stable, their facilitation more effortless.

## 4. Progression Architecture

- **Entry:** Two-person cooperation with an NPC one altitude away. Simple shared task with clear role differentiation.
- **Mid:** Three-person team spanning three altitudes. The player must translate between NPCs who cannot directly understand each other.
- **Advanced:** Five-person holonic team with emergent conflicts between altitude-levels. The player must facilitate resolution while maintaining the group's productive capacity.
- **Mastery:** Full holonic ecology — NPCs at all altitudes, some growing, some regressing, the player holding the entire developmental field while the group accomplishes complex multi-stage objectives.

## 5. Drive Probes

| Drive | Probe |
|-------|-------|
| Eros | Does the player facilitate upward growth in the team — creating conditions for NPCs to develop? |
| Agape | Does the player genuinely serve lower-altitude NPCs — meeting them fully without condescension? |
| Agency | Does the player maintain their own centre and contribution while facilitating others? |
| Communion | Does the player create genuine group coherence — a felt relational field, not just task coordination? |

Dual-domain scoring: vertical axis evaluates whether the player navigates altitude differences in the team (Eros/Agape); horizontal axis evaluates whether the player balances personal contribution with group facilitation (Agency/Communion).
