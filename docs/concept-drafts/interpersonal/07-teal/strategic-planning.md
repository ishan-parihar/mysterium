# Interpersonal × Turquoise — Strategic-Planning

## 1. Core Mechanic

**Planning Integral Relational Life.** The player designs and maintains a relational architecture across time — structuring cross-altitude relationships, scheduling mentorship, allocating relational energy, and planning developmental support for others while sustaining their own growth edge.

Three task families:
- **Relational Portfolio Design:** Given a set of NPCs at various altitudes with various needs, the player allocates relational energy across time. Scored on whether the portfolio serves both the player's growth AND others' development.
- **Mentorship Sequencing:** Plan a developmental trajectory for a lower-altitude NPC — what relational interventions, in what order, over what timeframe. Scored on developmental appropriateness and pacing.
- **Boundary Scheduling:** Plan when to open boundaries (communion/growth) and when to consolidate (agency/integration). Scored on whether the rhythm supports sustainable integral relating rather than burnout or stagnation.

## 2. Shadow Surfacing

- **DA (Relational Architect):** Over-plans relational life — every interaction scheduled, every relationship mapped into a system. The plan becomes a substitute for spontaneous connection. High structure, low aliveness.
- **DAll (Authentic Pluralist):** Refuses to plan relationally — 'relationships should be organic,' 'scheduling connection is inauthentic.' Plans that avoid altitude-differentiation, treating all relationships as equivalent.
- **GA (Premature Merger):** Plans that skip developmental stages — scheduling deep communion before building attunement capacity. Timelines that assume instant relational depth.
- **GAll (Integral Relational Fortress):** Plans that are perfectly structured for attunement but never include boundary-opening phases. Sustainable but growth-capped — the plan maintains current capacity without risking expansion.

## 3. Catalyst → Experience → Integration

- **Catalyst:** Planning scenarios where the player must balance multiple relational demands across time. No plan can satisfy all needs simultaneously — trade-offs are inherent.
- **Experience:** The player confronts their relational planning style — do they over-structure, under-structure, bypass stages, or avoid risk? The plan reveals the shadow.
- **Integration:** Iterative planning with feedback — the system shows consequences of previous plans, and the player refines their approach. Over time, plans become more integral: structured yet alive, boundaried yet open.

## 4. Progression Architecture

- **Entry:** Simple two-relationship portfolio. Plan relational energy allocation across one week. Binary feedback: did both relationships receive appropriate attention?
- **Mid:** Five-relationship portfolio across multiple altitudes. Plan must differentiate approach by altitude while maintaining coherence.
- **Advanced:** Dynamic portfolio — NPCs grow and change altitude over time. Plans must adapt to shifting relational landscapes.
- **Mastery:** Full relational ecology — 8+ relationships, mentorship chains, boundary rhythms, and the player's own growth edge all integrated into a living plan that evolves session to session.

## 5. Drive Probes

| Drive | Probe |
|-------|-------|
| Eros | Does the plan include growth edges — scheduled risks, boundary-openings, encounters with higher-altitude others? |
| Agape | Does the plan include genuine service — time allocated to mentorship and support without expectation of return? |
| Agency | Does the plan protect the player's own centre — rest, consolidation, boundaries that prevent relational burnout? |
| Communion | Does the plan create genuine relational depth — not just maintenance but deepening of connection over time? |

Dual-domain scoring: vertical axis evaluates whether plans navigate altitude appropriately across time (Eros/Agape); horizontal axis evaluates whether plans balance self-care with relational investment (Agency/Communion).
