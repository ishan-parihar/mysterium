# Interpersonal × Turquoise — Scenario-Choice

## 1. Core Mechanic

**Integral Relational Dilemmas.** The player faces branching scenarios where cross-altitude navigation is required — situations where relating to one person at their altitude creates tension with another, where maintaining centre conflicts with deepening connection, where mentorship demands sacrifice of comfort.

Three scenario families:
- **Cross-Altitude Conflict:** Two NPCs at different altitudes are in conflict. The player must relate to BOTH at their respective levels while facilitating resolution without imposing a single frame.
- **Mentorship vs. Autonomy:** An NPC at a lower altitude is making a choice that will cause suffering. The player must decide how much to intervene — too much violates Agency, too little violates Agape.
- **Boundary Dilemmas:** A situation demands deeper communion than the player's current relational boundary allows. Choose: open further (risk premature merger) or hold boundary (risk fortress).

## 2. Shadow Surfacing

- **DA (Relational Architect):** Choices that prioritise mapping the conflict over engaging it — selecting 'analyse the dynamic' over 'enter the dynamic.' The system tracks when the player chooses observation over participation.
- **DAll (Authentic Pluralist):** Choices that flatten altitude — treating both parties as equals when the situation demands altitude-differentiated responses. Selecting 'everyone's perspective is equally valid' when it isn't.
- **GA (Premature Merger):** Choices that leap to unity — 'let's all just come together' without doing the work of understanding each party's actual position. Bypassing conflict through performed harmony.
- **GAll (Integral Relational Fortress):** Choices that maintain perfect attunement but refuse vulnerability — the player navigates the dilemma flawlessly but never risks their own boundary. Selecting the 'safe integral' option over the 'vulnerable integral' option.

## 3. Catalyst → Experience → Integration

- **Catalyst:** Dilemmas are designed so that no single shadow-pattern produces optimal outcomes. The Architect's analysis fails because the situation demands presence. The Pluralist's flattening fails because altitude matters. The Merger's unity fails because differences are real. The Fortress's safety fails because growth requires risk.
- **Experience:** The player feels the genuine difficulty of integral relating — it is not a technique but a lived capacity that requires all four drives in balance.
- **Integration:** Repeated exposure to dilemmas where only genuine integral relating succeeds builds the capacity through practice, not instruction.

## 4. Progression Architecture

- **Entry:** Two-party dilemmas with clear altitude differences. The 'correct' choice is identifiable through integral logic.
- **Mid:** Multi-party dilemmas where the correct choice depends on reading the relational field, not applying a formula.
- **Advanced:** Dilemmas with no clean resolution — the player must choose which value to prioritise and live with the relational consequences.
- **Mastery:** Emergent dilemmas generated from the player's own shadow profile — the system creates scenarios that specifically target the player's weakest relational edge.

## 5. Drive Probes

| Drive | Probe |
|-------|-------|
| Eros | Does the player choose growth-oriented options even when they create relational discomfort? |
| Agape | Does the player choose to meet others at their altitude even when it requires descending from their own? |
| Agency | Does the player maintain their own values and centre even when relational pressure pushes toward collapse? |
| Communion | Does the player choose genuine connection even when it requires vulnerability and boundary-risk? |

Dual-domain scoring: vertical axis evaluates whether choices navigate altitude appropriately (Eros/Agape); horizontal axis evaluates whether choices balance self-maintenance with genuine joining (Agency/Communion).
