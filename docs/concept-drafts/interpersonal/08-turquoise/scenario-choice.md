## 1. Modality Identity

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Module | Interpersonal / White |
| Modality | Scenario-Choice |
| Unique Lateral | Non-dual relational dilemmas — situations requiring communion without fusion |
| Core Mechanic | Branching relational scenarios where no choice preserves a fixed self/other boundary |

## 2. Task Architecture

2.1 **Communion-Without-Fusion Dilemmas:** Scenarios where the player must act relationally without either merging (losing distinction) or separating (maintaining boundary). The 'correct' response holds both simultaneously.

2.2 **Transparent-Field Scenarios:** Situations where multiple beings need something and the player cannot act FROM a fixed position. Acting as the field rather than as an agent within it.

2.3 **Relational Paradox Scenarios:** Situations where helping requires not-helping, where connecting requires not-reaching, where communion arises through apparent non-action.

## 3. Shadow Detection Mechanics

| Shadow | Choice Pattern |
|---|---|
| 2.1 Grasping-at-Other | Choices that fix the other: 'I'll help THEM specifically', personalising the field |
| 2.2 Aversion-to-Other | Choices that withdraw: sovereignty-language masking avoidance, 'they need to find their own way' |
| 2.3 Relational Bypassing | Choices that perform unity: 'we're all the same' without honouring actual difference |
| 2.4 Identity-with-Relater | Choices that centre the relater: 'I'll be the bridge', maintaining connector-identity |

## 4. Scenario Categories

4.1 **The Dissolving Partner:** A long-term NPC companion begins losing fixed identity. Player's response reveals grasping-at-other or genuine field-transparency.

4.2 **The Unwanted Communion:** Relational configuration arises that player would normally avoid. Response reveals aversion-to-other or genuine openness.

4.3 **The Unity Test:** Situation where claiming oneness would be easy but inauthentic. Response reveals bypassing or genuine communion.

4.4 **The Invisible Bridge:** Situation requiring relational action without anyone knowing who acted. Response reveals relater-identity or genuine transparency.

## 5. Choice Architecture

5.1 No choice is labelled 'correct'. All choices are relationally coherent at some altitude.

5.2 Choices are scored on a multi-dimensional vector: transparency, authenticity, communion-depth, field-awareness.

5.3 The system tracks choice-patterns across scenarios to identify residual grasping signatures.

## 6. Catalyst → Experience → Integration

6.1 **Catalyst:** Scenarios designed to activate specific residual patterns — the dilemma IS the catalyst.

6.2 **Experience:** The felt-sense of being unable to resolve the dilemma from a fixed position — the discomfort of boundary-dissolution.

6.3 **Integration:** Choices that arise from field-transparency rather than from a positioned self. The system detects whether the choice was effortful (compensated) or spontaneous (integrated).

## 7. Scoring

- Multi-vector scoring: no single 'right answer'
- Pattern-across-scenarios weighted higher than individual choices
- Shadow-signature detection requires minimum 4 scenario-completions
- Drive-residual scoring from choice-language and timing

## 8. Implementation Notes

- Scenarios must be genuinely paradoxical — no hidden 'correct' answer
- NPC behaviour must be sophisticated enough to create authentic relational fields
- Choice timing is diagnostic: rushed choices suggest bypassing, frozen choices suggest grasping
- Scenarios regenerate with variation to prevent memorisation
