# Emotional / Turquoise — Strategic-Planning

## 1. Core Mechanic

Planning the integral emotional life. This modality probes the player's capacity to strategically structure their relationship with affect across developmental altitudes — scheduling equanimity practice, managing cross-level emotional demands, allocating emotional resources across systems, and designing personal protocols for maintaining integral affect under real-world conditions. The probe: can vision-logic emotion be intentionally cultivated, or does the player rely on spontaneous capacity alone?

### 1.1 Task Structure

- **Phase A — Emotional Resource Mapping:** The player is presented with a complex life-scenario involving multiple emotional demands at different altitudes (a grieving friend, a workplace conflict, a spiritual community, a family system). They must map the emotional resources required for each and identify where depletion risks exist.
- **Phase B — Equanimity Architecture:** The player designs a practice structure — when to engage, when to withdraw, how to maintain sovereign centre across competing emotional demands. The plan must honour all altitudes without collapsing into any single strategy.
- **Phase C — Disruption Response:** The plan is disrupted by unexpected emotional events. The player must adapt their strategy in real-time while maintaining integral coherence — neither rigidly adhering to the plan nor abandoning structure entirely.

### 1.2 Planning Dimensions

- Temporal allocation (when to engage which emotional system)
- Depth management (how much emotional permeability to allow in each context)
- Recovery architecture (how to restore equanimity after intense engagement)
- Cross-altitude sequencing (which emotional demands to address in what order)
- Boundary design (where sovereign centre requires protection vs. where openness serves)

## 2. Shadow Diagnostics

| Shadow | Planning Signature |
|--------|-------------------|
| DA (Cartographer) | Over-elaborate plans that map every emotional contingency but never include direct engagement; planning AS avoidance of feeling |
| DAll (Pluralist) | Refuses to prioritise emotional demands; plans treat all systems as equally urgent; no altitude-based sequencing; overwhelm through non-discrimination |
| GA (Premature Equanimist) | Plans that assume equanimity is already achieved; no recovery architecture; no acknowledgment of genuine emotional cost; "I'll just be present" |
| GAll (Fortress) | Excellent plans for engagement and holding but no plans for release or transcendence; recovery = restoration of holding capacity, never deepening |

## 3. Catalyst → Experience → Integration

- **Catalyst:** The planning demand forces the player to confront the reality that integral affect requires intentional cultivation — it is not merely a state that arrives. Strategic thinking about emotion reveals where the player's actual capacity ends and performance begins.
- **Experience:** The felt-sense of emotional sovereignty as a practice rather than a trait — something that requires architecture, maintenance, and honest self-assessment. Planning emotion is itself an integral-emotional act.
- **Integration:** Over time, the player develops genuine emotional life-architecture — not rigid control, but wise stewardship of their affective capacity across the full complexity of their relational world.

## 4. Drive Probes

| Drive | Probe Mechanism |
|-------|----------------|
| Eros | Does the plan include growth edges — deliberate exposure to emotional territory beyond current capacity? Or does it only maintain current level? |
| Agape | Does the plan include return — time allocated to holding space for lower-altitude emotional needs in self and others? |
| Agency | Disruption response — does the player maintain strategic coherence under pressure or abandon sovereignty? |
| Communion | Does the plan extend beyond personal emotional management to include stewardship of group-level emotional fields? |

## 5. Progression Arc

- **Entry:** Simple two-demand scenarios; binary planning choices; mild disruptions. Player learns emotional resource-thinking.
- **Midpoint:** Multi-system scenarios with altitude diversity; complex planning with temporal and depth dimensions; moderate disruptions requiring real-time adaptation.
- **Mastery:** Full life-complexity scenarios; plans that integrate personal, relational, and systemic emotional demands; severe disruptions that test the plan's integral coherence.
- **Shadow Mode:** Targeted planning challenges — e.g., "plan your emotional engagement for today" for DA (forces concrete commitment), "which demand is most important?" for DAll (forces hierarchy), "what will you do when equanimity fails?" for GA (forces honesty), "plan your next release" for GAll (forces transcendence-orientation).
