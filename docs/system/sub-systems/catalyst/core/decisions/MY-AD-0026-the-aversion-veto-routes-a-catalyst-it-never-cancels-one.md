## Context

`45 §4` holds that personalization shapes **how** catalyst is delivered and never **which**
development is served. It admits exactly one suppression — the aversion veto (`45 §5.2` rule 3) — and
left the conflict rule unstated. The unstated case is the one that matters: the aversive candidate is
the only carrier of the catalyst the developmental state requires. Two opposite failures were
available and neither was chosen: forcing the encounter (violating a stated boundary), or dropping the
target (letting a preference quietly override the developmental agenda — the comfort trap `45 §4`
names).

## Decision

A catalyst target is a **cell** (`24 §2.1`: line × stage × modality × shadow quadrant); a candidate is
one **rendering** of that cell. The veto therefore operates on renderings:

- another rendering of the same cell exists → it is offered, and the veto is invisible to `24`;
- no other rendering exists → the cell is **deferred and recorded** with the veto that caused it,
  never forced;
- the veto names a whole cell (a safety/re-traumatisation condition) → `45 §5.2` rule 1 territory,
  routed to the safety layer, not adjudicated by the scheduler.

This is a **routing** rule: it changes the candidate set `24` sees, never the cell the decision above
it chose. `46 §5.3` inherits it — the dialectic engine may not treat an aversive domain as the
"opposite pole to be trained".

## Consequences

- Positive: a player's stated boundary is never negotiable by an algorithm, and the developmental
agenda is never silently cancelled — the two failure modes are both excluded rather than traded.
- Positive: the deferral is itself diagnostic material (a cell that stays deferred *is* the material),
which is why it is recorded rather than skipped.
- Negative: **an open edge remains** — nothing owns how long a deferral may stand, or whether the
healing path (`16 §10.4`, `43 §4.7`) is obliged to pick it up. Recorded in `45 §10` as still open.
